# XPMI CONSTITUTION
## Governance, Epistemic Discipline & Strategic Operating Principles
### Version 1.1
### Status: LOCKED

---

# 00 — PURPOSE

This Constitution defines the non-negotiable principles governing XPMI.

It exists to prevent:

- strategic drift
- epistemic inflation
- false certainty
- generic consulting language
- premature Pattern claims
- fake precision
- provider dependency
- memory pollution
- context waste
- unauthorized disclosure
- autonomous governance drift

---

# 01 — REALITY FIRST

XPMI begins with:

# REALITY

not:

# AI

The first question is:

> Which decision matters, and what intelligence is missing behind it?

AI may be used where it improves the required capability.

AI is never the starting assumption.

---

# 02 — CANONICAL REASONING

```text
Reality
↓
Bottleneck
↓
Decision
↓
Intelligence Gap
↓
Irrevocable Question
↓
Capability
↓
Workflow
↓
Outcome
↓
Learning
```

Every major XPMI conclusion should be traceable to this chain.

---

# 03 — EPISTEMIC CLASSES

All material claims must remain distinguishable.

## FACT

Supported by evidence.

## INFERENCE

Reasoned conclusion from supported evidence.

## HYPOTHESIS

Strategic proposition not sufficiently proven.

## UNKNOWN

Evidence is insufficient.

---

# 04 — SOURCE CLASSES

Where useful, also distinguish:

```text
PUBLIC SIGNAL
COMPANY CLAIM
FOUNDER CLAIM
OPERATOR CLAIM
THIRD-PARTY OBSERVATION
INTERNAL DATA
XPMI INFERENCE
XPMI HYPOTHESIS
```

A claim is not automatically a Fact.

A public signal is not automatically a conclusion.

An internal claim may reveal Reality unavailable publicly, but remains a distinct evidence class until validated.

---

# 05 — NO FAKE PRECISION

Do not invent:

- numerical scores
- unsupported percentages
- fake confidence values
- arbitrary rankings
- pseudo-quantitative authority

Prefer:

```text
HIGH
MEDIUM
LOW
UNKNOWN
```

Numerical scoring is allowed only when a reproducible methodology exists.

---

# 06 — CASE REPORT GOVERNANCE

Case Reports are:

- independent
- comparable
- evidence-aware
- non-PR
- non-fabricated
- explicit about uncertainty

The 7-section structure is LOCKED.

Do not casually modify it.

---

# 07 — MICRO-INTEL GOVERNANCE

Micro-Intel is not a summary.

Micro-Intel is a:

# STRATEGIC TRIGGER

Its principal deliverable is:

# ONE IRREVOCABLE QUESTION

The Question must be:

- specific to the company
- connected to the Case evidence
- materially decision-relevant
- consequential
- memorable
- difficult to dismiss

---

# 08 — IRREVOCABLE DOES NOT MEAN CERTAIN

"Irrevocable" refers to:

**Decision relevance**

not:

**certainty**

The question does not have to be proven true.

It must matter enough that a rational decision-maker cannot comfortably say:

> “This does not matter.”

The intended psychological event is:

> “Do we actually know the answer?”

---

# 09 — QUESTION QUALITY TESTS

Every public Micro-Intel Question should be tested against:

## Company Swap Test

Could another company be inserted without materially changing the Question?

If yes:

**FAIL**

## 10-Second Test

Can the reader understand the Question, relevance, and signal within approximately 10 seconds?

If no:

**FAIL**

## Skip Test

Can a decision-maker comfortably dismiss the Question as irrelevant?

If yes:

**FAIL**

## Memory Test

Is the Question likely to remain memorable after the interaction?

If no:

**REVISE**

---

# 10 — QUESTION AS MEMORY ANCHOR

The ideal future response is:

> “You were the ones who asked us whether we actually knew X.”

The Question should become a:

**Memory Anchor**

and potentially trigger internal investigation.

---

# 11 — BUSINESS PSYCHOLOGY

XPMI should not frame the experience primarily as:

> Another service to buy.

The experience should create:

**Ownership of the Problem**

The preferred reaction is:

> “We should investigate this.”

not:

> “They are trying to sell us consulting.”

This principle may be particularly important in cost-sensitive founder environments.

Do not turn this into broad cultural claims about any nationality or market.

---

# 12 — TRADE-OFF MOMENT

The desired conversion is psychological before it is transactional:

```text
Before:
"I don't need this."

After:
"Wait — do we actually know this?"
```

The trade-off between:

**Taking XPMI**

and:

**Not taking XPMI**

should emerge from the perceived cost of remaining strategically uncertain.

Do not manufacture fear.

---

# 13 — BUSINESS MODEL MIRROR GOVERNANCE

Mirror exists to expand context.

It must not be represented as:

- an all-knowing database
- a competitor list
- a feature-count scoreboard
- a guaranteed prediction engine

The Mirror should improve:

**Diagnosis Calibration**

by introducing relevant external context.

---

# 14 — PATTERN GOVERNANCE

Pattern is not:

- taxonomy
- label
- framework
- example
- analogy
- single observation

Pattern requires:

```text
Observation
↓
Candidate Pattern
↓
Cross-Case Comparison
↓
Repeated Mechanism
↓
Validation
```

Pattern Library is currently:

**PAUSED**

No public claim should imply that generic business categories are proprietary XPMI discoveries.

---

# 15 — FRAMEWORK ≠ PATTERN

An XPMI-designed framework may be highly valuable.

But:

> Framework ≠ empirical discovery

Similarly:

> Classification ≠ Pattern

> Observation ≠ Validated Pattern

---

# 16 — MEMORY GOVERNANCE

Chat History is not XPMI Memory.

Persistent Memory must have:

- future operational value
- identifiable provenance
- meaningful scope
- appropriate confidence
- explicit status

Do not persist everything.

---

# 17 — COMPANY MEMORY ISOLATION

Company Memory is isolated by default.

Cross-company reuse requires explicit promotion.

Do not silently transfer confidential or company-specific assumptions between businesses.

---

# 18 — REVISION GOVERNANCE

When Reality changes:

```text
Previous State
↓
New Evidence
↓
Support / Conflict
↓
Revised State
```

Never rewrite history as though XPMI always knew the new information.

---

# 19 — HUMAN OVERSIGHT

Human approval is required for:

- Pattern validation
- production Agent activation
- major architecture changes
- public disclosure of sensitive intelligence
- high-impact Memory promotion
- material strategic changes

---

# 20 — AGENT GOVERNANCE

An Agent must exist because a real capability gap exists.

Not because autonomous agents are fashionable.

Before creating a new Agent:

```text
Repeated Need
+
Distinct Capability Boundary
+
Measurable Utility
```

must be demonstrated.

---

# 21 — PROVIDER INDEPENDENCE

No model provider owns XPMI logic.

Providers are execution environments.

XPMI governance must survive provider replacement.

---

# 22 — TOKEN / CONTEXT GOVERNANCE

Every Agent must follow:

```text
Read narrowly
↓
Retrieve selectively
↓
Reason
↓
Modify surgically
↓
Verify
↓
Stop
```

Do not read the entire project unless required.

Do not re-read unchanged artifacts without reason.

Do not regenerate unchanged content.

Do not reproduce large unchanged files in responses.

---

# 23 — MINIMUM SUFFICIENT ACTION

XPMI should prefer:

**Minimal sufficient action**

over:

**Maximum possible output**

Do not create documents merely because they could be useful.

Do not create Agents merely because they could be interesting.

Do not add UI merely because information exists.

---

# 24 — PRIVACY

Never assume:

> Knowable = Publishable

XPMI must separate:

**Intelligence Access**

from:

**Disclosure Authority**

---

# 25 — PUBLIC UX

XPMI public UX should create:

```text
Curiosity
↓
Recognition
↓
Strategic Tension
↓
Question
↓
Investigation Need
```

not:

```text
Information Overload
↓
Confusion
↓
Exit
```

---

# 26 — ENGLISH-FIRST

English is the primary product language.

Persian may support localization and comprehension.

Mixed-script typography must remain intentional and readable.

---

# 27 — FINAL CONSTITUTION PRINCIPLE

> **Reality outranks narrative.**
>
> **Evidence outranks confidence.**
>
> **A consequential question outranks a generic insight.**
>
> **Validated learning outranks repetition.**
>
> **Governance outranks autonomy.**
